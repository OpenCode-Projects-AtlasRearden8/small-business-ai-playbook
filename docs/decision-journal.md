# The decision journal

Big choices have a way of looking obvious in hindsight, which is exactly why hindsight is a liar. The fix is cheap: write down the decision, the reasoning, and how you'll know if it worked — **before** you find out.

AI's role here is small on purpose: it helps you sharpen the writing and poke at the reasoning. It never makes the decision.

## The format (5 minutes per entry)

Keep it in a single file, a notebook, or one note per decision:

```
DATE:            2026-XX-XX
DECISION:        One sentence. What I'm doing.
WHY:             Two or three sentences. The real reason, not the story.
OPTIONS I SAID NO TO:  e.g. (a) ..., (b) ...
CONFIDENCE:      1–5 (1 = a coin flip, 5 = dead sure)
CHECK BY:        A date. Usually one month out.
SUCCESS LOOKS LIKE:  Something measurable, however small.
FEELING:         Optional, but honest. "Nervous but ready" is fine.
```

That's the whole system. The last field matters more than it looks — six months from now, "how did I feel when I chose this" is information you can't recover any other way.

## Rules that keep it honest

- **Write it before the outcome.** An entry made after you know the result is a story, not a record.
- **"Why" beats "what."** You'll always remember what you did; you won't remember why.
- **One sentence per option you rejected** — enough to remember the alternative existed when the same choice reappears.
- **Confidence is a number, not a vibe.** Numbers can be graded later.
- **The "check by" date is the payoff.** Each money-date, flip open the journal and grade anything past its date.

## Grading past entries

When an entry comes due, spend three minutes asking:

1. **What happened?** Facts, not narratives.
2. **Was my reasoning right for the right reason?** Right result through wrong reasoning is a warning sign, not a win.
3. **What did my "success looks like" say?** Met, missed, or unmeasurable?

## Where AI helps

- **Sharpening:** "Here's a decision journal entry. Rewrite the 'why' in one crisper sentence without softening it."
- **Pressure-testing:** "Play devil's advocate: what would a cautious businessperson question in this reasoning?" Use its questions to add to the entry — don't let it change your answer.
- **Pattern review, quarterly, once or twice a year:** "Here are three of my past decisions with outcomes. What pattern am I repeating that I seem unable to see? Be gentle but direct."

## Where AI must NOT go

- Never let AI produce a *recommendation* and then engrave it. A journal entry reading "AI suggested..." isn't your reasoning. Rewrite it: "I chose X because..."
- Don't paste identifying business or personal details. Summaries, amounts, and dates are enough for every prompt above.
- Don't outsource the confidence rating. Only you know it.

## Why this compounds

One-person business owners usually repeat the same five hard decisions wearing different costumes (raise the price? do the collab? buy the tool? wait it out?). A decision journal turns each costume into a lesson you already paid for. It pairs beautifully with the [money-date](money-date.html): entries with a check-by date in the past get graded there.
