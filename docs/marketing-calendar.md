# The marketing calendar

Most one-person businesses don't need a marketing strategy. They need **a rhythm** — something predictable enough that you stop arguing with yourself about whether to post, and simple enough to survive a busy month.

## The 3-3-1 rhythm

Each week, aim for three touchpoints across three kinds of content, plus one batch-block work session:

| Slot | What kind | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1a | **Helpful** — teaches or solves something | A plain-English tip from your actual domain |
| 1b | **Human** — shows the person behind the work | What you're working on, a lesson learned |
| 1c | **Offer** — what you sell, stated plainly | "This is my book, here's who it's for" |
| 2 | **Batch block** — one 45–90 min session, once a week | Draft all three; schedule, don't improvise daily |

The offered content is the one people skip out of discomfort. Don't. Three helps to every one direct offer is the honest ratio — helping people is allowed to lead to sales.

The rhythm repeats weekly and never gets cleverer than this. Cleverness is what strategies die of.

## The seasonal layer

Beyond the weekly rhythm, sketch the year once. Four anchor moments is enough to start:

- **Q1:** A "new year, simple plan" piece (this is when your audience most wants simple)
- **Mid-year:** A bigger artifact — a guide, a worksheet pack, a bundle
- **Holiday season:** Whatever makes a natural gift or year-end wrap-up
- **Your own anniversary:** The month your LLC or your first product turned 1 — easiest authentic story all year

One-person businesses don't do Black Friday fireworks well unless they've been planning since September. It's fine to sit those out; anchor moments beat marketing holidays.

## Where AI helps (drafting only)

- Turn one idea into week's content: *"Here's a topic: [topic]. Draft three micro-posts: one helpful, one human, one offer. Factual claims marked so I can verify."
- Recycle honestly: *"Here's a post that worked: [paste]. Suggest two genuinely different angles on the same idea."*
- Trim: *"Cut this announcement to 80 words without losing the warmth."*

## Where AI hurts

- **Invented facts.** If AI drafted a statistic, a quote, or a number, verify it before it ships, every time, or cut it.
- **Tone drift.** AI quotas toward the median brand voice, which is public radio with a discount code. Your [voice rules](emails.html#the-tone-target) apply to marketing too.
- **Volume traps.** The calendar that says "post daily" will be dead by February. The 3-3-1 survives February.
- **Unreviewed auto-scheduling.** Never let "AI wrote it" flow straight to "scheduled." AI-written is fine — AI-left-unread is not.

## The monthly 15-minute calendar review

Attach this to the [money-date](money-date.html) (or any first-of-month moment):

1. What shipped this month? Count it. (3-3-1 is four items — did you hit 60%? Great.)
2. One thing that got a response? Note *why* in a line, then plan a variant.
3. Anything on the seasonal layer due this month or next?

That's it. Fifteen honest minutes beat a marketing masterclass.
