# The monthly money-date

One hour a month, once a month. That's the entire small-business money routine described here — not because money is simple, but because the *ritual* can be.

## A note on the numbers

This chapter is a **ritual**, not a bookkeeping course. Where you actually record income and expenses — the ledger itself — lives in the companion project **cashflow-simple-ledger**. That project explains how to track the money; this page explains the habit of sitting down with it, what questions to ask once a month, and how to let AI act as a calm second brain instead of a fortune teller.

If you don't yet have a ledger set up, do that first (any simple expense/income log works, ledger or spreadsheet), then come back. The money-date works with whatever tool you already use.

## What a money-date is

A monthly appointment with yourself: same day each month, one hour, one drink of something nice, one worksheet. You look at what happened last month and make a handful of calm decisions about the next one.

Pick a date that recurs naturally: the 1st, the last Saturday, the day after your bank statement posts. Put it in your actual calendar as a repeating event named something you won't dread, like "coffee with the books."

## The 60-minute agenda

| Minutes | Step |
|---|---|
| 0–10 | Settle in. Open your ledger, last month's worksheet, and this page. Phone in another room. |
| 10–25 | **Read the ledger.** Income, expenses, what came in, what went out. No changes — just read. |
| 25–40 | **Ask the six questions** (below). Write answers on your worksheet. |
| 40–50 | **AI second pass.** Have AI review your answers (method below). Challenge it back. |
| 50–60 | **Pick three moves** for next month and write them down. Done. |

## The six questions

These are the whole meeting:

1. **What came in?** (Income by source — books, services, reselling, whatever you have.)
2. **What went out?** (Any expense that surprised you gets a star.)
3. **Anything owed or due?** (Bills, taxes already set aside, subscriptions about to renew.)
4. **What earned its keep?** (Which product or task actually produced money or saved you hours?)
5. **What dead weight do I keep carrying?** (Tools, subscriptions, habits that didn't produce anything.)
6. **What are the three moves for next month?** (Small, concrete, doable. "Cancel X," "List Y," "Ask Z" — not "grow revenue.")

## How AI can help — and exactly how far

AI is a good **reviewer** and a bad **accountant**. Use it like this:

**Good uses during the date:**

- Ask it to rephrase a muddled answer into a clear next step:
  > "Here are my three goals for next month: [paste]. Rewrite each one as a single concrete task with a verb."
- Ask it to spot patterns in *descriptions* you wrote (which tasks dragged, what you dreaded):
  > "These are my notes from the last month, [paste]. Group them into: things that made money, things that cost time, and things I'd stop doing."
- Sanity-check a decision journal entry: "Here's a decision I made and my reasoning. What would a cautious person poke at?"

**Never do these:**

- Don't paste account numbers, tax IDs, or identifying financial details.
- Don't ask AI to calculate taxes, fees, or anything where a wrong number costs real money. It can be confidently wrong. Your ledger or a calculator does math; AI tells you what to look at.
- Don't let it generate "financial advice." You're looking for cheerfulness and clarity, not stock picks.

## After the date

- File the worksheet (paper or folder). Old money-dates become the best record of how the business actually behaved.
- Anything recurring you discovered (a subscription to cancel, an invoice to chase) goes on **today's** to-do list, not next month's.
- Let the AI reviewer see each new worksheet only after personal and financial identifiers are removed — amounts and dates are fine; account numbers are not.
