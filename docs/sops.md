# Simple SOPs

An SOP (standard operating procedure) sounds corporate. It isn't. In a one-person business it's just **the checklist for the thing you secretly keep re-learning.**

You already have SOPs even if you never wrote them — they're in your muscle memory. That's exactly where they're fragile. Writing them down takes twenty minutes once and saves you each time your brain is foggy, tired, or nine months past the last time you did the thing.

## What deserves an SOP in a one-person business

Only a few kinds of tasks earn a checklist:

- **Infrequent but important** — publishing a book chapter, renewing a business filing, seasonal reselling prep
- **Easy to get wrong** — anything with real money, real deadlines, or steps done out of order
- **Brain-foggy-day tasks** — the ones you'd skip "just this once" unless there's a list waiting

Everything else lives free. Six SOPs is plenty. If everything has a checklist, nothing does.

## The format

```markdown
# SOP: [Task name]
LAST CHECKED: 2026-XX-XX
WHEN TO USE: The trigger. "Nth of month," "when a review arrives," etc.

STEPS:
- [ ] Step one (concrete: what tool, what button)
- [ ] Step two
- [ ] Done when: [definition of done]

GOTCHAS:
- The step past-me always forgets goes here.

FILES IT TOUCHES: where outputs live (folder name only, no personal paths)
```

That last section — **GOTCHAS** — is the soul of an efficient SOP. It's the fine detail you can't reconstruct from a fresh brain.

## Rules that keep SOPs small

- **Maximum 10 steps.** Longer usually means two SOPs.
- **Concrete, not descriptive.** Not "update the listings," instead: "open the app X → tab Y → edit price."
- **No step you won't actually do.** If a step never happens, either the SOP lies or the task has changed.
- **A "done when" is required.** Half-finished SOPs drain more energy than no SOP at all; a finish line fixes that.

## Where AI helps — and its limits

- **Turning rambles into checklists (great):**
  > "Here are my rough notes about how I do [task]. Turn them into a numbered checklist a tired version of me could follow, max 10 steps. Flag anything ambiguous as a question."
- **Spotting missing steps (useful):**
  > "This is my checklist for [task]. What step might a beginner forget? Suggest questions, not extra steps."
- **Writing SOPs for tasks you've never done (dangerous):** AI will invent plausible-sounding procedures for things nobody on your side has done. An SOP written without a first real run-through is fiction with checkboxes. Only codify a process after you've done it once by hand.

Gotcha: AI output tends to add steps to make the process sound more rigorous. Prune anything you wouldn't do on a foggy day.

## The living update

An SOP is a standing document. Whenever a step goes stale:

1. Do the step the **new** way on purpose.
2. Update the SOP **same day** (it takes a minute).
3. Update `LAST CHECKED`.

Twice a year (bonus points for piggybacking a money-date), skim all your SOPs top to bottom in one sitting and mark the ones still accurate. Old, wrong SOPs are worse than no SOPs — they sound just as official.
